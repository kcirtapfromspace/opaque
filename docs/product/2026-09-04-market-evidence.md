# Opaque: market evidence register

Checked **September 4, 2026**. This register supports the product strategy; it is not a product evaluation or security certification. Sources below are vendor documentation, official announcements, or original research. **Documented** means the source describes a capability. No competing product was installed, adversarially tested, or independently benchmarked. Dates distinguish announcements from undated documentation checked today. Missing documentation does not establish a missing capability.

The market evidence challenges a broad positioning around secret invisibility, identity, policy, approval, and audit. Multiple products cover that combination. It also challenges treating stateful authorization as an unoccupied category. Opaque needs a workflow customers adopt and an enforcement advantage it can demonstrate against their actual alternatives.

## Differentiation hypothesis and counterpoints

The strongest hypothesis is **a usable grant-to-effect lifecycle for a specific sensitive task**: authorize limited work, enforce the limit across clients and retries, and reconcile what actually changed. Its possible advantage is the combination of trusted transaction review, deterministic constraints, durable accounting, revocation, and provider-aware recovery. Each element must earn its place through customer evidence.

For example, a production investigation could permit selected reads, then authorize deployment of one reviewed commit once. A convincing demonstration would preserve useful completion while denying target substitution, replay, session resets, delegated overspending, and duplicate execution after a lost response. Compare setup effort and approvals per completed task as well as attack resistance. Customers may reasonably prefer configuring their incumbent; measure that alternative rather than assume replacement demand.

Research already explores this boundary. [AID-Guard, August 21](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21159), studies approval-to-effect binding, reservations, and recovery under specified provider contracts. It supplies relevant prior art and evaluation scenarios, not evidence of market adoption. Opaque should not claim this architecture is its invention. Nor should it promise universal exactly-once effects: reliable recovery depends on target-system semantics and evidence of whether a request took effect.

Independent evidence is another hypothesis. A signed receipt may help investigations before anyone needs a transparency service. Merkle inclusion proves membership in a log; it does not independently prove that every effect was logged or that a provider performed the represented action. Validate the buyer's verification requirement before prioritizing public-log infrastructure.

## Standards and transferable research

[OIDC-A](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25974), dated September 30, 2025, is an individual proposal. Its title does not make it an adopted OpenID Foundation standard. The [Foundation's October 7 whitepaper announcement](https://openid.net/new-whitepaper-tackles-ai-agent-identity-challenges/) is a separate source. Base integrations on established identity mechanisms while tracking proposals.

MCP's official [Enterprise-Managed Authorization extension](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/extensions/auth/enterprise-managed-authorization), announced stable [June 18, 2026](https://blog.modelcontextprotocol.io/posts/enterprise-managed-auth/), is an integration option. The specification says extensions are opt-in and client support varies. Do not present EMA as a universal enterprise requirement or assume every chosen client implements it. Confirm compatibility in each pilot.

The attached Polsia dossier compares **Polsia with Adanima**, not Opaque's direct market. Its useful transfer is the hypothesis that visible routines, approvals, limits, and completed work make autonomy understandable. The [primary homepage](https://polsia.com/) supports its company-running positioning; this review did not independently establish the dossier's revenue, churn, user, or review figures. None should support Opaque's market sizing or differentiation claims.
